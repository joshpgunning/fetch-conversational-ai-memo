Fetch in Conversational AI: Options

Getting Fetch into the chat, without asking anyone to remember we exist.

Chatbots are becoming the new browser. This memo is the options-and-risks read Gowtham asked for, to hand to Wes before we decide whether this is worth a real proposal.

Author Joshua Gunning Contributors Gowtham Gundu, Steve Hollinger Team Fetch Labs (Innovation) Status Options memo, no build committed Last updated August 18, 2026
If you read one thing

People are starting to do their planning, research, and shopping inside ChatGPT and Gemini instead of a browser tab. The ask: can Fetch ride along — surfacing a relevant point or offer mid-conversation — without the user ever having to remember Fetch exists?

That last clause is the whole constraint. It has to survive on a one-time setup, not something a user does per session, because "remember to invoke Fetch" is the exact problem we're trying to solve in the first place. Three ways to get there exist today; none of them are ready in the way we'd want, and the data on how often this would even matter is thinner than the idea deserves. This memo lays out the options, what's actually blocking each one, and the honest size of the opportunity — so you're working from real numbers instead of a vibe when you weigh whether this earns a real proposal or stays a "stay-ready" bet.

01

Why this, why now

Wes, Ori, and Gowtham have all independently landed on the same read: as chat assistants replace the browser tab for research and planning, Fetch's absence from that surface is a growing gap, not a static one.

The concrete case that keeps coming up: a user asks ChatGPT to plan a trip to Florida. It builds the itinerary — and somewhere in there, could it also say "if you book this through Fetch right now, you'll earn N points"? Same shape applies to CPG and restaurant decisions, the areas where Fetch actually has data on the user.

To be clear about what this is not: it is not an attempt to make the purchase happen inside Fetch, or to turn ChatGPT into a new checkout surface for Fetch. Both would be a multi-year behavioral-change problem — the kind that's historically been brutally hard to move, even from inside our own app. The ask here is narrower and more opportunistic: nudge the sliver of value Fetch already has, at the moment it's relevant, in a surface we don't own.

02

The constraint that rules out most ideas

Two questions were on the table, deliberately in this order:

1

Can we make Fetch context "stick" in a session without the user re-adding it every time? Right now, in ChatGPT, even a linked doc has to be re-attached per chat. A rewards nudge that requires that kind of manual re-invocation every session isn't solving the top-of-mind problem — it's just relocating it.

2

Do we have any data suggesting this is relevant today, or is this a bet on a future state? (Section 04 below.)

One idea floated and set aside: asking users to add a small snippet to their own custom system instructions ("if this query is about shopping, pull context from this Fetch link"). Technically light — but it asks a user to go edit their own AI's system prompt for our benefit, which is a much bigger ask than it sounds. The read in the room: conversion on that would be close to zero, and we'd be building real infrastructure to support a behavior almost nobody will do.

05

Other risks

Discovery is the real blocker, not injection.
Even if Fetch context is technically available, nothing today on ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini decides on its own to go get it. Every option above either requires the user to set something up first, or waits on a discovery layer nobody has shipped.
Adjacent legal terrain.
The most promising passive signal — inferring an in-flight purchase from a receipt or confirmation email — runs into the same restrictions Fetch's eReceipts work is already navigating with legal around email review. Nothing here should be pitched as "we'll just read the inbox" without that context attached.
The mobile gap.
Most people don't chat on desktop. Jumping straight to the Gemini or ChatGPT phone app is probably the norm, not the exception — and none of the three options above reach that path today.
06

Foundational context: the Rewards Layer (not this memo's ask)

Separately, the offsite produced a bigger, longer-horizon bet: turning Fetch's issuance, identity, fraud, and redemption infrastructure into a rewards layer other companies could subscribe to — wrapped in the same MCP surface, and related to (but distinct from) the UCP track. That work is why an MCP endpoint (option B above) isn't a from-scratch build if we ever need it — it's a byproduct of a track that's already moving.

One thread worth noting only in passing: Robin has raised PepsiCo's interest in using Fetch as rails for their own in-app targeting more than once — early signal that "Fetch as a service" has real pull beyond this specific chat-assistant use case. That belongs in the Rewards Layer conversation, not this one.

Whichever way this goes, three things I'd hold onto regardless:

→Don't chase the custom-system-instructions idea — the ask is too heavy for the conversion it would generate.
